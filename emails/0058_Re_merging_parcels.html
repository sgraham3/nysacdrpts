Good morning -

I have a follow-up question related to a question I had asked previously.

The backstory - A property owner had requested via a parcel combo form for my tax mapper to combine two adjacent parcels, and she denied it twice because the ownership was not the same.

On parcel A - The son is the sole owner. On parcel B - The son is the owner and the mother has Life Use.

Now the son filed a deed stating that the intention of the deed is to merge the two parcels.

So, my previous question was - Would you merge them? The lawyer is pushing the issue looking for tax law for why we can't.

At the time of my question, one of you (thank you) sent me this Op of Counsel

Volume 5 - Opinions of Counsel SBEA No. 118

How I read that is that the assessor could make the determination whether to allow the parcels to be merged on the role, though this would be for assisting with assessing and tax bill purposes, and NOT in

determination of legal ownership. Ok... fine with that.

Now the lawyer states that the intention is also for the mother's Life Use to carry to the newly MERGED parcel, even though it states nothing about her extending Life Use in the deed to the newly merged

parcel. In fact, she is not mentioned in the deed and did not sign it.

On top of that, the parcel with the house is NOT currently the one the mother has Life Use on. Instead, the son lives there and has a Basic STAR on it. (The other parcel is a vacant lot.)

Would you put the Life Use on the new parcel? The only thing that the new deed states is that the FATHER's Life Use was extinguished because he passed away, but nothing about the mother's life use.

Even if it was put on, and if the mother is living there, too - and if she applied for an Enhanced STAR, it would have to be a credit, not an exemption. What a cluster.

I would appreciated any thoughts. This lawyer is being... persistent.
